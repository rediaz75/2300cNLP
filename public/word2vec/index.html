    Word Analogies

    Word2Vec famously encodes relationships as directions. A − B + C finds the word whose vector is closest to that sum: "A is to B as ? is to C."

    This is a small model trained on 7M words, not the 100B-word Google News set, so analogies work best with common words. Try king − man + woman, france − paris + tokyo, or good − better + bad.

    Settings

    Trained with training/train_word2vec.py in this repo. Rerun it to retrain or change the hyperparameters.

    1 · Skip-gram

    For every word in the corpus, the network learns to predict the words within 5 positions of it. Words that appear in similar contexts end up with similar vectors.

    2 · Cosine similarity

    Every vector is normalized to length 1, so similarity = dot product = cos θ. The "10 closest" are the 10 highest cosines out of 20,000 words, computed live in your browser.

    3 · PCA to 3D

    100 dimensions can't be drawn, so the map projects the plotted words onto their top 3 principal components. Distances in 3D are approximate; the table shows the true cosine scores.

    4 · Corpus bias

    A model only knows its training text. Gutenberg includes the King James Bible, so king sits near biblical kings, and Reuters makes money sound like a central banker.
